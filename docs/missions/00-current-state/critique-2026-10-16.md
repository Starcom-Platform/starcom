# The owner's critique of 16 October 2026

The owner walked the live app from a first visit, on desktop (M2 Mac mini) and
on a phone (iPhone XS). This record keeps every point as a task, in the
owner's words where they matter, with what is already known and whose it is
likely to be: **Starcom**, **Mecha Jono** (the data or reports published), or
**both**. "To find out" means nothing has been checked yet. Each item gets
its own decision record when it is taken up, as K1–K18 did after the first
critique (`critique-audit.md`).

The rule for anything that is the intel's fault rather than Starcom's (C23):
the owner must be told, and Mecha Jono must be sent a URL artifact for that
case, so their systems can be upgraded.

## What works, and must not regress

- The first load of the 3D globe: "it's fuckin' amazing".
- Space Weather: the magnetic field lines, the plasma particles (heliosphere
  and near Earth), the aurora, the electric field over the US and Canada.
- Weather's temperature visualization.
- The solar system view: the Sun and the star system's effects.
- Intel Reports' click-through and report narratives, where they work.
- Comm Hubs ("awesome").
- An iPhone XS runs the globe well.

## Built, CR3 (2026-10-16)

- **C5, a click on an Intel Report opened the wrong report, or nothing.** The globe's hover handler is
  memoised and read `pickLayer` and `reportAt` without depending on them, so once reports arrived after
  mount, a glyph's index was read against an earlier list: on starcom.app a click on a mark in Greece
  opened Hurricane Isaias, placed in Kentucky. Measured: 11 popups opened from 12 clicks, none of them the
  report under the cursor. Read through refs now (`Enhanced3DGlobeInteractivity.tsx`); on the build every
  probed click opened the report under the cursor. The rest of C5 is D-CT's open list (satellites,
  eco-disaster marks, cyber threats, Conflict Zones tallies, analysis marks, arcs).
- **C7, the hover left over a popup.** A click on the globe, or the pointer leaving it, now ends the
  point tooltip (`useGlobePointTooltips.ts`), and the country's tooltip is not shown while a popup is open
  (`useCountryInfoOverlay.ts`): with the first fix alone, the country under the mark showed through.
- **Also seen:** a hover is not picked again while the pointer is still and the camera moves (a tour, a
  deeplink's flight), or while a layer redraws under it: what is named can be what was there before, and
  the click (picked afresh) opens what is there now. Seen once in the eco probe below. Small; not fixed.
- **C5, earthquakes, fires and floods** (D-CT's open list, the first of its layers). A click opens the
  event as its feed states it, under its hazard's name: the title, "Reported by" its feed, its time, what
  the feed says (magnitude, depth and USGS's review status; a fire's burned area in acres; GDACS's alert
  level), and its page at the publisher: USGS's event page, the EONET source's page (IRWIN's incident, the
  Smithsonian's volcano), GDACS's report. Nothing is restated that the feed did not say: the severity
  bucket that colours the mark is Starcom's, so it is not in the note. `screenPoints.ts` and
  `useScreenMarkLayer.ts` now carry `opens`, as `intelReportSprites.ts` does, for the layers after this.
  Two wrong labels found and fixed on the way: USGS's tsunami flag was shown as "Tsunami warning issued"
  (USGS is not a warning centre and the flag is not a warning: "USGS tsunami flag set" now, the note
  pointing to tsunami.gov), and every EONET fire was credited to IRWIN while about half of them, 3,855 of
  7,148 on 16 October, are GDACS's (each is named by its own sources now). Probed on the build: four
  quakes, a Red cyclone and two fires each opened their own note and link; Escape closed it; nothing sent.
  A catalogue volcano drawn over a quake takes the click and opens nothing, as D-CT decided for readings.
- **C5, the rest of D-CT's point layers.** Satellites open their CelesTrak record (name, NORAD number,
  altitude drawn, "predicted, not observed"; the country guessed from a name is not shown). A cyber threat
  cluster opens how many of the country's listed addresses each threat list names in the sample drawn,
  with each list's file, and never an address: a listed address is often someone's compromised device.
  A Conflict Zones tally opens the country's 25 newest GDELT events in Diplomacy's catalogued words
  (CAMEO's code and name, Goldstein and its caveat, the coding caveat) with each report; a displacement
  ring opens UNHCR's figures by country of asylum, linked to the API row they came from (UNHCR's own code,
  "GFR" for Germany, which its filter takes). CAMEO's conflict names come from GDELT's own lookup, kept
  apart from the codebook's cooperative names, since GDELT's text differs from those on 8 of 79. An
  analysis mark opens what Starcom's analysis concluded and how: sources counted, window, weight, what
  computed it, its blind spots, a fixture said first. Left on D-CT's list: the arcs (react-globe.gl's).
- **Found on the way: a click at a mark's rim opened nothing** though the hover said "Click to open". The
  hover reads the pointer in whole pixels and the click in fractions; at the rim the click fell a pixel
  past the pick (measured on a Conflict Zones tally: the hover hit at 604, 518; the click at 604.76,
  518.94 hit nothing). A click that picks nothing within 3 px of where the hover read a mark's notes now
  opens them; a click on another mark still opens that one (`useGlobePointTooltips.ts`).
  Probed on the build, each layer's hover found by moving the pointer and its click read back: a cyber
  cluster (CHE, Blocklist.de's file), a tally (EGY, the 25 newest of 32, each with its report), a
  displacement ring (Syria, UNHCR's row) and a satellite (PROGRESS-MS 34, NORAD 68837, its SATCAT record);
  each closed by Escape, no tooltip left, nothing sent. Two things seen: a country's events often share a
  title (the same pair and code, from different reports), so the list cannot tell them apart; and
  CelesTrak refuses its "active" group (403) to a network that downloaded it within two hours, so after
  several probes from one browser with no copy kept, satellites are not drawn and the layer says why. The
  satellite probe read CelesTrak's stations group in its place. Live (5b793c66) the same four passed.
  Mutations: 32, each bit (the commit says 27: miscounted). The newest event the live tally opened for
  Egypt was GDELT's coding of a celebrity fashion article: the coding caveat and the report link are
  there for exactly that.

- **C4, loading states:** decided as D-LS (LS1–LS4, all the recommended picks) after measuring all 28 layers:
  5–22 s between first paint and the first mark, with only the key's small "loading…" meanwhile.
  `docs/missions/01-architecture-decisions/loading-states.md`.
